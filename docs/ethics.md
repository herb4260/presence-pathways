# Ethics, safety, and researcher reflexivity

## Core position

Presence Pathways brackets metaphysical judgment. It studies how experiences become real, meaningful, distressing, healing, or durable in people's lives without reducing them either to divine proof or psychiatric symptom.

## Human-subjects requirements

Before collecting real data:

- obtain institutional ethics approval;
- use informed consent that distinguishes research from pastoral or clinical care;
- explain limits of confidentiality and procedures for imminent safety concerns;
- minimize collection of names, exact places, congregation identifiers, and third-party details;
- separate contact information from research data;
- encrypt identifiable data and never commit it to Git;
- define retention, deletion, withdrawal, and controlled-access procedures;
- compensate participants without making participation coercive.

## Distress and support protocol

Interviewers should respond to content, agency, distress, function, and risk—not to unusualness alone. A vivid experience may be supportive; a familiar religious form may still be coercive or harmful.

Pause and follow the approved safety plan when a participant reports imminent risk, commands to harm self or others, inability to care for basic needs, severe sleep loss, escalating fear, loss of control, or marked functional disruption. The research team should have supervised referral pathways that respect both clinical and spiritual supports chosen by the participant.

The public demo does not implement automated risk prediction.

## Community power

Researchers should ask who has authority to name an experience, whose interpretation is doubted, and whether testimony is rewarded, pressured, or silenced. Community recognition can support integration, but it can also constrain what participants feel safe to report.

## Reflexivity statement template

The researcher should update a living memo addressing:

- personal religious or nonreligious commitments;
- professional training and its default explanations;
- experiences that create strong identification or skepticism;
- power differences arising from institutional, clinical, or professional roles;
- expectations about healing, trauma, and transformation;
- moments when participants revise the researcher's categories.

Reflexivity is not a confession that invalidates research. It is an auditable account of how knowledge is produced.

## Public repository rule

Only synthetic data are permitted in this repository. Real transcripts, exact quotations, audio, raw fieldnotes, participant IDs, and linkage keys must remain outside GitHub in an approved secure environment.

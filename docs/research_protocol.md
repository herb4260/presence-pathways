# Research protocol: Presence Pathways pilot

## Purpose

This pilot asks how experiences interpreted as divine presence or grace become psychologically consequential and how change is sustained, revised, or contested over time. It combines long-form ethnographic attention with a structured longitudinal layer.

This repository is a research-design prototype, not an approved human-subjects study.

## Design

| Phase | Timing | Data | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orientation interview | Day 0 | 60–90 minute interview | Learn participant vocabulary, biography, practice history, and community context |
| Experience sampling | Days 1–21 | One brief evening prompt daily | Observe within-person variation in practice, presence, body, emotion, meaning, community, distress, and functioning |
| Participant observation | Throughout | Fieldnotes from services, prayer meetings, study groups, and ordinary social settings | Understand how practices are taught and experiences are interpreted publicly |
| Midpoint interview | Around Day 10 | 30–45 minute interview | Reconstruct events while still recent and refine participant-specific prompts |
| Follow-up interview | Day 28–35 | 60–90 minute interview | Distinguish peak intensity from persistence and integration |
| Interpretive feedback | After preliminary analysis | Participant/community consultation | Test whether interpretations are recognizable without treating agreement as the only validity criterion |

## Sampling strategy

Use purposive, maximum-variation recruitment rather than a statistically representative sample. A pilot might recruit 25–40 adults across several Korean Christian communities, with variation in age, gender, practice style, congregational role, and prior familiarity with experiences described as grace or presence.

Recruitment language should welcome people with intense, subtle, positive, ambiguous, absent, or distressing experiences. Sampling only dramatic healing accounts would bias the project toward transformation narratives.

## Unit of analysis

The project distinguishes four levels:

1. **Moment:** What happens in a particular episode?
2. **Person:** How does the episode differ from that person's usual day?
3. **Relationship and community:** How do other people supply practices, language, validation, doubt, or care?
4. **Cultural model:** What shared assumptions about mind, personhood, suffering, and divine action make an experience intelligible?

## Data collection principles

- Use the participant's terms before introducing researcher categories.
- Record sensory quality, relational quality, agency, context, and consequence separately.
- Ask about change at multiple time horizons.
- Seek negative cases and experiences that did not lead to relief.
- Treat fluctuation and ambiguity as findings, not measurement failures.
- Keep clinical distress questions available without making diagnosis the frame of every interview.

## Fieldnote template

Each fieldnote should separate:

- **Observed scene:** who, where, actions, sequence, words, sensory environment.
- **Participant interpretation:** terms and explanations offered by participants.
- **Researcher response:** emotion, bodily response, assumptions, positionality.
- **Analytic questions:** competing explanations and missing evidence.
- **Follow-up:** people or scenes to revisit, with privacy safeguards.

## Pilot success criteria

The pilot succeeds if it shows that:

- participants can answer the EMA items in their own terms;
- prompts capture meaningful within-person variation without excessive burden;
- interview and fieldnote material changes how numerical patterns are interpreted;
- the safety protocol is usable and non-stigmatizing;
- at least one initially plausible mechanism is revised by negative-case analysis.

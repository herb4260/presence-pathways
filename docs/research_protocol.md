# Research protocol

## Working title

**Presence Pathways: A longitudinal mixed-methods study of felt divine presence and psychological change**

## Aim

To study how experiences interpreted by participants as divine presence or grace unfold in everyday life and how attentional, emotional, relational, narrative, and communal processes are associated with psychological change over time.

The study does not adjudicate metaphysical truth claims and does not assume that spiritual experiences are inherently therapeutic or pathological.

## Phase 1 — field engagement

Before finalizing measures, conduct participant observation and informal conversations to learn local vocabulary, practices, relationships, expectations, and forms of authority. Use field engagement to revise, add, or remove quantitative items.

## Phase 2 — 21-day experience sampling

Prompt participants once each evening. Measures include felt presence, practice, embodied warmth, tears, peace, feeling loved, gratitude, worry relief, narrative coherence, community connection, distress, functioning, sense of control, and sleep.

The bilingual demonstration instrument is in `instruments/ema_items_en_ko.csv`.

## Phase 3 — phenomenological interviews

Conduct interviews at midpoint and after the sampling period. Reconstruct context, first noticed change, bodily sensations, attention, emotion, perceived relationship, interpretation, community response, function, and what persisted or faded.

## Phase 4 — mixed-method integration

Quantitative analysis compares each participant with their own average level. Qualitative analysis preserves sequence, meaning, contradiction, and negative cases. Joint displays connect trajectories with interview scenes and researcher memos.

## Data security

The public repository is for code, instruments, synthetic data, and aggregate examples only. A real study should store contact information, consent files, audio, transcripts, fieldnotes, and analytic linkage keys in approved encrypted systems with role-based access.

## Safety

High intensity alone is not a crisis marker. Attend to distress, control, sleep, functioning, coercion, and risk. A real protocol should specify supervised pathways for clinical and pastoral support and procedures for imminent safety concerns.
